Planetary defence

Near-Earth object follow-up & discovery

Most newly found near-Earth objects (NEOs) are seen on just one night by a large survey before they fade or move out of reach. They are posted on the Minor Planet Center's NEO Confirmation Page (NEOCP), and an orbit can only be secured if other observatories measure them again within hours. That is my main job: with the 1.0-m f/2.9 telescope at Tičan (MPC code L01) I recover NEOCP candidates, measure their positions (astrometry) and report them to the Minor Planet Center, often as the first station to confirm a new object. Along the way we also co-discover new NEOs.

Since 2017 Višnjan has become one of the most productive follow-up stations in the world. According to MPEC Watch, in 2025 L01 was 5th in the world for discovery follow-up circulars and 1st in Europe. My own observations appear in 1,125 Minor Planet Electronic Circulars, placing me #112 among 6,009 individual observers credited in MPECs since 1993.

Among the 1,118 objects I have observed are 31 comets, including the interstellar comet 3I/ATLAS, which we measured on the night of its discovery (1 July 2025, MPEC 2025-N12), 13 potentially hazardous asteroids, and metre-sized bodies such as 2025 PU1 (H = 31.4). Every one of them is plotted in an interactive 3D map of the Solar System, together with statistics and the method behind the ranking.

Solar physics

Solar physics & high-resolution solar imaging

During the day the observatory's work continues on the Sun. Image quality depends on the air above the telescope, so I measure the daytime seeing at Višnjan Observatory with a solar scintillation monitor, and I keep developing new ideas for high-resolution imaging, making the most of the remarkably steady air over the northern Adriatic.

Robotic observatory commissioning

Robotic observatory software

I develop robotic observatory software, and I implement new software and hardware by running a fully remote robotic observatory at Tičan.

Since December 2023 I have been responsible for its technical integration and commissioning: integrating, configuring and validating the optical, mechanical, network, storage and control-system components so that they operate together as one robust platform, and developing the procedures for remote operation, fault monitoring, system recovery and safe unattended operation. The software stack spans N.I.N.A., MaxIm DL, Tycho Tracker, PixInsight, Zabbix, Grafana and observatory-specific scripts.

A technical review paper describing the observatory is coming soon.
